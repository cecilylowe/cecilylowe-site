Cecily Lowe

My name is Cecily Lowe. I am a Ph.D. student in physics at Yale University, where I work in David Moore’s lab at the Wright Laboratory. My field of research is levitated optomechanics: I build sensors out of silica microspheres and nanoparticles trapped with light in vacuum, and use them to study the interaction between dark matter and ordinary matter.

Before Yale I studied physics and data science at the University of California, Berkeley.

Professional Work

At Yale I lead the microsphere array experiment: a two-dimensional array of optically levitated microspheres, each one a force sensor, built to search for dark matter through the tiny momentum kicks it would give ordinary matter. An array lets the experiment reject noise that every sphere feels at once, and catch interactions too rare for a single sensor.

In 2025 I was awarded a National Science Foundation Graduate Research Fellowship and a Marshall Plan Foundation Fellowship. Through the Marshall Plan fellowship I spent four months in Uroš Delić’s lab at TU Wien, developing a nanoparticle loading technique for the science goal of coupling a neutral Cs atom with a silica nanoparticle for the first time, to create non-Gaussian states of nanoparticle motion.
